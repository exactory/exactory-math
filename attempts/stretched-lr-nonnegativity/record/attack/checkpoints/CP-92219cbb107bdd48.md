## Exact statement added since the predecessor, and the correction it makes

Measured, not proved, and computed by regrouping records already written, with no new run.

Move 12 concluded that the driver of the per-facet margin is the number of negative edges on the
facet and not the size of the cell. That wording was too strong. Both effects are real and they
compose:

- The count grows with the cell. By edge-count band the maximum count is 1, 3, 7 and 6, the mean
  is 1.00, 1.10, 1.63 and 1.77, and the share of facets carrying three or more negative edges is
  0.000, 0.001, 0.139 and 0.201.
- The count still moves the margin with the cell's size held fixed. Inside the 512-edge band the
  median ratio is 589.29, 432.29, 266.13, 234.25, 187.78 and 135.76 for counts one to six.

So the count is the proximate driver and the cell's size acts through it. The sizes of the effects
are unchanged: the mean count grows about as the edge count to the power 0.35 and the median ratio
falls about as the count to the power 0.85, so a facet whose margin reaches one needs a count of
order two thousand, and a cell with that mean count would need of order ten million edges against
the 1,127 built. On this evidence the per-facet inequality is robust and still unproved.
