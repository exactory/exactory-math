## Exact statements added since the predecessor

1. A discharging rule turns the rung into a local statement. Give each positive-weight edge an
   equal share of its weighted length to each negative-weight edge in a chosen neighbourhood of
   it, and let `share(e)` be what `e` receives. The shares of distinct negative edges are disjoint
   parts of the positive sum, so `share(e) >= |w(e)|` times the lattice length of `e`, for every
   negative edge, gives `l >= 0` on every cell at once, with no cell enumerated. Both sides are
   linear on a cell, so each test is decided at the extreme rays.
2. The vertex neighbourhood, the edges sharing a vertex with `e`, is too small. Status:
   `certified-special-case-check-5`, `result.json` pass. It fails 106 of 14,899 ray tests, and the
   failure is sharp: at the worst ray the share is zero, so every edge meeting the negative edge
   at a vertex has zero length there while the negative edge does not. What keeps the coefficient
   nonnegative is positive weight further away, so no argument that looks only at the edges
   through a vertex can work.
3. The facet neighbourhood, the edges lying on a facet through `e`, is enough on every test made,
   and by a wide margin: 0 failures of 14,899, the share exceeding the demand by a factor of at
   least 1567265857/18738000, about 83.6, and by up to about 26,977. This is the neighbourhood the
   class of `e` already names, a class being the set of facet normals through the edge.
4. The statement this suggests, which has no cell and no size in it: for every five-part hive
   polytope and every edge whose class has negative weight, the shared weighted length of the
   positive-weight edges on a facet through it is at least `|w|` times its lattice length. It
   implies the rung by the disjointness of the shares.
