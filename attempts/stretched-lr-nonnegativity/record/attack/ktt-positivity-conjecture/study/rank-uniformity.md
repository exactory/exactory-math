# What the rank-five work gives every rung, and what it does not

Date: 2026-09-05. A derivation, with no computation in it. It audits the results proved in the
child attack `ktt-positivity-length-five` for dependence on the rank, and states how much of a
rung is left once Ferudun's theorems are applied, as a function of the rank.

## Notation

Write `n` for the number of parts, `D(n) = (n-1)(n-2)/2` for the number of interior entries of a
hive of side `n`, and `H` for the hive polytope, so `dim H <= D(n)`. Write `N_n` for the set of
primitive outward normals of the rhombus inequalities at side `n`. The construction in
`tools/hivepoly.py` writes three families of rhombus rows, of `n(n-1)/2` rows each, so there are
at most `3 n (n-1) / 2` rows and hence `|N_n| <= 3 n (n-1) / 2`. At `n = 5` this bound is 30 and
the true count is 27.

## Rank-uniform

Each of the following was proved in the child attack, and each is a statement about a rational
polytope and its lattice. None of them uses `n`, so each holds at every rank.

1. **The cell reduction.** On a cell of the chamber complex, where the polytope keeps every
   incidence, each vertex is a fixed linear function of the boundary triple and each edge keeps
   its transverse cone while its lattice length is linear. So `a_1` is a linear functional there,
   and `a_1 >= 0` on the whole cell holds exactly when it is nonnegative at the cell's extreme
   rays. The cells are finite in number, a cell being the finite datum of which rows are tight at
   which vertex.
2. **The nonnegative-class lemma.** Lattice lengths are nonnegative on the cell, so a cell all of
   whose edge classes have nonnegative weight satisfies `a_1 >= 0` with no computation.
3. **The discharging rule.** The shares of distinct negative edges are disjoint parts of the
   positive sum, so a local domination statement implies `a_1 >= 0`.
4. **The per-facet identity.** Every edge lies on `c(f)` facets, so giving each facet through `f`
   the amount `w(f) len(f) / c(f)` distributes `w(f) len(f)` exactly; summed over the facets the
   shares are the whole functional, and the per-facet inequality implies `a_1 >= 0`.
5. **The intrinsic-lattice contraction.** For a hive polytope of intrinsic dimension `d < D(n)`
   the stretched polynomial is the Ehrhart function of a `d`-dimensional rational polytope in a
   rank-`d` lattice, so the non-full-dimensional case is the same problem one dimension lower.

The same holds for every coefficient, not only `a_1`: nothing in 1 to 5 is about the index.

## Not rank-uniform

The atlas. The transverse cone of a face is generated by a subset of `N_n`, and the weights of
those cones are what the argument needs. The codimension-five atlas of `enumeration-run-1` is a
list of cones spanned by `N_5`, of no use at any other rank, and the cost of building the
analogous list grows with the number of normals and with the codimension.

## How much of a rung is left, as a function of the rank

Ferudun 2026 proves the top four coefficients of a full-dimensional hive at every rank, and
`a_0 = 1` always. So a full-dimensional hive of side `n` leaves the coefficients

    a_1, ..., a_{D(n) - 4}

open, which is `D(n) - 4` coefficients, and the coefficient `a_k` needs the weights of the
transverse cones of codimension `D(n) - k`, so the codimensions needed run up to `D(n) - 1`.

| `n` | `D(n)` | open coefficients | highest codimension needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 3 | none | none |
| 5 | 6 | 2, and one of them, `a_2`, is settled by the rank-five codimension-four computation | 5 |
| 6 | 10 | 6 | 9 |
| 7 | 15 | 11 | 14 |

So the rungs do not merely get bigger; they get harder in a way that is quadratic in the rank. At
rank five the whole rung is one coefficient and one codimension. At rank six it is six
coefficients and codimensions four to nine, in a space of ten dimensions, with a larger normal
set and with no closed formula for the weights above dimension three.

## What this means for the root

Proving the rank-five rung would close one rung of infinitely many and would leave the ladder
open, as the index already records. This derivation adds that repeating the rank-five method is
not a plan for the ladder: the reductions carry over unchanged, but the data they consume grows
quadratically in the rank in the number of coefficients and in the codimension, and the weight
computation has no closed form above dimension three. A route to the root therefore needs either a
rank-uniform proof of the per-facet inequality, which items 1 to 4 make a meaningful target, or a
different reduction that does not require an atlas per rank.

This derivation proves no coefficient nonnegative at any rank.
