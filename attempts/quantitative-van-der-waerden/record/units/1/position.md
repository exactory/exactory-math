# Position of unit 1

**On the path or off to the side.** On the path. The claim of the attack is a deficiency term for
the van der Waerden inequality, and this is the first inequality in the record, or in print, that
carries one: it is exact for uniform columns and strictly larger otherwise.

**Prior best it improves.** Two published bounds. Gurvits degree bound, cap(q) >= g(#supp v) after
one peeling (arXiv:0711.3496, Theorem 4.10), is recovered from it by Jensen and is strictly weaker
for every column not uniform on its support. The Knopp-Sinkhorn bound per(B) >= m_n on the face
with a zero entry (1982) is its corollary: a zero in column j gives K(v) >= g(n-1) by Jensen on
the other coordinates.

**What it does not do.** A single column cannot give the claim: Theorem 1 is an equality at sparse
uniform columns for every completion of the matrix, so no bound of the form gamma_{n-1} F(column)
exceeds about 0.6/n in the constant (route-capacity.md, section 5). The next rung needs two
columns at once, which is unit 6.
