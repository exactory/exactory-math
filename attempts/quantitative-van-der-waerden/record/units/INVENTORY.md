## ladder-the-parameter

- move 2 (pass 1, make-the-bound-explicit-then-attack-the-lossiest-step, paid bound_quality): Two new theorems, proved in study/route-capacity.md. Theorem 1: for every doubly stochastic B and every column j, per(B) >= gamma_{n-1} prod_i (1 - b_ij)^(1 - b_ij); the proof is two applications of the weighted AM-GM inequality to the polynomial obtained by peeling x_j once (Lemma A, cap(q) >= K(v)), followed by the standard Gurvits inequality for that polynomial (Lemma B, per(B) >= gamma_{n-1} cap(q), cited to Gurvits Corollary 2.5 and Laurent-Schrijver Theorem 1). It is sharp at every column uniform on its support, strictly refines the Gurvits degree bound, and contains the Knopp-Sinkhorn face bound m_n as a corollary. Theorem 2: with Lemma C, K(v) >= g(n) exp(||v - 1/n||^2/2) from phi(t) = (1-t) log(1-t) having phi'' >= 1, per(B) >= gamma_n exp(max_j d_j / 2) >= gamma_n exp(||B - J_n/n||_F^2 / (2n)). So the claim holds with c = 1/(2n). The rung climbs from 0 to 1/(2n), short of both the needed 1/n and the true 1/4: bound quality is what the move pays, since a single column cannot give more than about 0.6/n (Theorem 1 is an equality at sparse uniform columns for every completion), so the next rung needs two columns at once.
- move 3 (pass 1, choose-the-auxiliary-weight-or-certificate, paid object): At n = 3 the sharp claim Q(1/4) is proved: per(B)/gamma_3 - 1 - ||E||_F^2/4 equals the sum over the three rows b of B of 1 - 4 e_2(b) + 9 e_3(b), an exact polynomial identity on the doubly stochastic matrices, and each summand is nonnegative by Schur (e_1^3 - 4 e_1 e_2 + 9 e_3 >= 0 with e_1 = 1); equality exactly at J_3/3 and at the row and column permutations of (I + C_3)/2. At every n >= 5 a local theorem: per(B)/gamma_n - 1 >= [n/(2(n-1)) - 2n/(3(n-1)(n-2))] ||E||^2 - sum_{k>=4} beta_k ||E||^k with explicit beta_k, so Q(1/n) holds for ||E||_F <= 0.110 at n = 7 and within radius about 0.1 up to n near 30. Exact structure recorded: the Taylor coefficients are r_k(nE)/(n)_k with r_3 = (2/3) sum (nE)^3 and an explicit r_4; and a correction to the brief, max ||E||_F^2 = n - 1. The certificate rung is climbed at n = 3 only; object is what the move pays, because the n = 3 identity is a statement about one dimension and the local theorem about a ball, not about the claim.
- move 4 (pass 1, make-the-bound-explicit-then-attack-the-lossiest-step, failure signal fired, paid bound_quality): The rung did not climb. V1 reduces to one explicit inequality, (I2): C_2(v, v_prime) K(v + v_prime) >= g(n) g(n-1) exp((d + d_prime)/2), where C_2 is the capacity of the two-column polynomial P_V(y) = sum over pairs of rows of per(V[S,:]) prod_{s in S} y_s over prod y_s^{w_s}; (I2) holds numerically at n = 4 to 7 with ratio 1 only at v = v_prime = 1/n. What was proved about it: the sequential forms are false (cap(q_{n-2}) >= g(n-1) e^{d_prime/2} cap(q_{n-1}) fails, ratio 0.967 at n = 4), so no second one-column step exists and the gain of a pair is a joint quantity; the minimiser of C_2 satisfies per-coordinate quadratics in two scalars, so log C_2 is an explicit function of two ratios; (I2) holds for disjoint supports and locally near (1/n, 1/n) with an explicit second-order margin, and its only tight direction is v_prime = 2/n - v, where K contributes nothing and the whole gain is 1 - <v, v_prime>. Why it did not close: every provable lower bound on the Hessian of the capacity goes through the smallest column entry, which on the n = 7 region can be 0.02, and the tight direction has relative margin 1/(n-1) while entry deviations on the region reach 100 percent, so neither a convexity floor nor a second-order expansion closes it. The failure signal fires: three moves without a higher value, and the remainder, (I2), is stated exactly. Bound quality is what the move pays: the constant stays at 1/(2n).

## prove-the-barrier-first

- move 5 (pass 1, axiomatise-the-method-and-build-a-near-miss, paid obligations): The barrier, stated. (i) The documented ceiling of every one-column argument is a = 1/(2n) up to a constant: Theorem 1 is an equality at sparse uniform columns for every completion of the matrix, so no bound of the form gamma_{n-1} F(column) exceeds about 0.6/n, and the interpolation route proves that every one-segment input leaves the endpoint free above exp(d^2/(2n)). (ii) The shared property: each stopped method uses information about one column, or one permutation, at a time, and the second-order deficiency is a joint two-column quantity of size 1/n, so any pointwise-in-permutation scheme gives a negative constant for n >= 5 and any one-column peeling caps at 1/(2n). (iii) The sequential two-column forms are false, with ratio 0.967 at n = 4, so the joint gain cannot be recovered one column at a time by any argument. (iv) The exact residue is one inequality, (I2): C_2(v, v_prime) K(v + v_prime) >= g(n) g(n-1) exp((d + d_prime)/2) for probability vectors with v + v_prime <= 1, where C_2 is the capacity of the two-column polynomial; it holds numerically with ratio 1 only at the centre, is proved for disjoint supports and locally near the centre, and its only tight direction is v_prime = 2/n - v. (v) Why the joint tools fail on it: the convexity-plus-Hessian floor goes through the smallest column entry (0.02 on the n = 7 region, a thirtyfold loss) and is genuinely degenerate for disjoint supports; every explicit dual weight is exact in one regime and first-order lossy in another; second-order expansion fails because the tight direction has relative margin 1/(n-1) against entry deviations of order one. What would remove the barrier is named exactly: a drop lemma C_2 >= (1 - <v, v_prime>) exp(-c_n |s|^2 - fourth order) with c_n <= 0.15 at n = 7, or a monotonicity argument in the two scalars of the C_2 optimiser. Obligations are what the move pays: (I2) is now the one statement the record owes for n = 7.
