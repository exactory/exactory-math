# Position of unit 5

**On the path or off to the side.** On the path, as the statement of where the path stops. It is
the barrier the whole attack converges on, and every route in `study/routes-rubric.md` reduces to
it.

**Prior best it improves.** Nothing in print, because the statement is about Theorem R, which the
sibling attack proved and which is unpublished. What it improves is the record's own picture of
the problem: at move 1 the binding constraint was identified, at move 7 the best available chain
was pushed from a threshold of `n >= 35` to `n >= 23`, and at move 8 the components were split so
that two of the three interfaces closed and the third was named exactly.

**The barrier, stated once.** A lower bound on `per(A)` that is strong exactly when `cap(A)` is
small, that is, when `A` is quantitatively near-decomposable. Every bound in the record degrades
in that regime, and the same gap sits on the capacity side of Gurvits and Leake, arXiv:1804.04351.

**Why it is a barrier and not a to-do item.** The dichotomy of move 6 is exhaustive: either
`cap(A) >= gamma_n / m_n`, in which case `per(A) >= cap(A) m_n >= gamma_n` closes the case, or
`-log cap(A) > log(m_n / gamma_n)`, and in that branch the permanent lower bound has to take over.
Unit 3 shows the permanent side has no room left at either extremal, so the bound has to come
from the capacity side, where no candidate in the record reaches below `n = 23`.
