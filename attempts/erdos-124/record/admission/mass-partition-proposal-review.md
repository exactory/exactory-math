## Label multiplicity and remaining proof obligations

Disjoint classes of base labels do not establish disjoint numerical power sets. Coincidences can occur within a class and across classes. Counting every occurrence in a prefix estimate and then applying a numerical-set theorem after deduplication would leave a substantive gap. So would treating numerical disjointness as if it followed from distinct bases.

The proposal explicitly requires the labelled extension to be proved before application and declares extension failure a stopping condition. This is an appropriate research obligation, rather than an additional assumed hypothesis. Its absence as an already proved lemma is not an admission objection.

The later proof must account for the following points, which remain unverified here:

1. Define the labelled supplies and permitted finite sums consistently through every imported argument, including collisions and bounded-gap estimates.
2. Justify the extension of the cited finite-support argument, with particular attention to tail separation and to each use of disjointness. Citation to the numerical-set statement alone is insufficient.
3. Keep the labels used for finite correction separate from the labels available for final amplification, and establish the required bounded-gap property after that correction support is removed.
4. Verify the finite partition at the stated inclusive mass threshold, preserving the correction class's collective gcd and all three required masses. A proof only for strict inequality would not match this proposal.
5. Retain every positive cutoff and every finite labelled deletion in the deficit, irrational-divergence, and modular arguments. Do not add pairwise coprimality, multiplicative independence, a base-size bound, or a uniform bound on deletions.

These requirements are already covered by the proposal's applicability, success, and failure language. They are future result-review requirements, not new conditions imposed on admission. Finite-deletion robustness is part of the exact scope; it is not established here as a separate source of novelty beyond the all-cutoff objective.
