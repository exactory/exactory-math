silently become legal pure powers in the present problem.

## Cantor-thickness lead

The final three queries identified Astels, Thickness measures for
Cantor sets (1999), the author's Waterloo thesis, and subsequent primary
work on sums of Cantor sets. The search result from the thesis states a
sumset criterion using sum tau_i/(1+tau_i), with a value at least one
yielding an interval. This is a source lead, not yet a full theorem
applicability audit.

Possible new strategy question: use the homogeneous digit Cantor sets
associated with each individual base, retain their actual per-base
self-similarity, and determine whether a REAL interval theorem gives
a sufficiently exact finite INTEGER seed with legal occurrence support.
The numerical resemblance of the thickness expression and the root's
reciprocal mass is a reason to study the route, not a proof.

The missing interface must be stated explicitly. Multiplying a sum of
fractional powers by a common scale does not generally preserve pure
powers for every base. Real interval coverage or bounded approximation
error does not imply exact eventual integer representation. A successor
must not disguise the old unproved central-seed claim behind terminology
or treat the refuted generic arithmetic profiles as a substitute for
this interface.

No new theorem, lower mass threshold, finite seed, or priority claim is
recorded by this triage. Admission and the full study are required before
executing a successor strategy.
