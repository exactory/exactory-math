## 1. What the classical amplification supplies

Graham, Complete sequences of polynomial values, Duke Mathematical Journal 31
(1964), 275-285, DOI 10.1215/S0012-7094-64-03126-6, Lemma 4, proves the following
fact. If a fixed positive integer h is a finite signed sum from every tail of
a sequence, its positive subset sums contain arbitrarily long progressions of
step h. The proof chooses each new signed identity beyond the supports already
used. Its negative and positive parts are alternative positive assignments.
For h=1 this gives arbitrarily long intervals.

The occurrence distinction matters. Lemma 1 of that paper combines a sequence
with bounded prefix deficits and a separate nearly complete sequence by
interleaving their occurrences. It does not reuse the same occurrences in both
summands. The pages containing the definitions, Lemma 1 and its proof, and
Lemma 4 and its proof were visually inspected in the saved source PDF.

Thus even proving that every pure-power tail admits a unit signed identity
would not, by this argument alone, verify the residual support inequality in
move 1. We do not claim that signed tail representability is insufficient for
all possible completeness arguments. Its adequacy by another argument has
not been decided here.

## 2. Fixed support cannot supply bounded nonzero differences in all tails

Let D be any finite set of integers greater than one. Fix positive integers r
and H. There exists K=K(D,r,H) such that no signed sum of at most r labelled
powers d^j, d in D, j>=K, has value c with 1<=|c|<=H.

Proof. Suppose a signed sum equals such a c. Remove any nonempty zero-sum
subcollection, repeating until no such subcollection remains. At least one
term remains, because c is nonzero. Write the remaining equation as

    sum_(i=1)^t (epsilon_i/c) d_i^(j_i) = 1,
    1<=t<=r, epsilon_i in {-1,1}.

For fixed t, c, ordered bases d_i and signs epsilon_i, the vector
(d_1^(j_1),...,d_t^(j_t)) belongs to the subgroup of (C*)^t generated by the
t vectors whose i-th coordinate is d_i and whose other coordinates are one.
This group has finite rank t. The remaining equation is nondegenerate because
all nonempty zero subsums were removed. The Evertse-Schlickewei-Schmidt theorem
on linear equations in finite-rank multiplicative groups therefore gives only
finitely many such vectors. Each vector determines its exponents, since d_i>1.
There are finitely many choices of t, c, ordered bases and signs, so there are
only finitely many remaining nondegenerate equations with their exponent
solutions. Choose K greater than every exponent occurring in these solutions,
or K=1 if there are no solutions. Any original equation supported in j>=K
would leave a nondegenerate equation supported there, a contradiction.

This proof includes degenerate original identities and numerical coincidences
between different labels. Removing a zero subsum introduces no new label.
It uses neither gcd(D)=1 nor the reciprocal condition. It gives no numerical
bound for K. The ESS bound on the number of solutions is not a height bound.

Source: Evertse, Schlickewei and Schmidt, Linear equations in variables which
lie in a multiplicative group, Annals of Mathematics 155 (2002), 807-836,
DOI 10.2307/3062133, arXiv:math/0409604v1, introductory main theorem. The saved
HTML and mathematical-text extraction were inspected. The statement above is
a corollary of that source, not a claimed novel Diophantine theorem.

In particular, any unit exchanges available in arbitrarily deep pure-power
tails must have unbounded support size. Bounded-size polynomial finite-
difference gadgets cannot be moved into arbitrarily deep fixed-D tails.
This does not exclude variable-length exchanges, which were the admitted
construction question.

## 3. Bounded differences lose their gain relative to support weight

There is a separate elementary cost fact. Let (P_t,Q_t) be infinitely many
pairs of disjoint finite occurrence sets from any locally finite positive
integer sequence. Suppose their unions are pairwise disjoint and

    1 <= h_t=sum(Q_t)-sum(P_t) <= H_0

for a fixed H_0. Set b_t=sum(P_t), B_s=sum_(t<=s)b_t,
H_s=sum_(t<=s)h_t, and W_s=2B_s+H_s as in move 1. Then

    B_s/s -> infinity,     H_s/W_s -> 0.

Proof. Only finitely many pairs can have P_t empty: each such Q_t has positive
sum at most H_0 and hence uses at least one of the finitely many occurrences
of value at most H_0. For every bound M, only finitely many of the remaining
P_t can contain an occurrence of value at most M, by local finiteness and
disjointness. Therefore b_t tends to infinity. Its Cesaro averages B_s/s tend
to infinity, while H_s<=sH_0. This proves the two limits.

For unit switches H_s=s and the chosen assignments represent [B_s,B_s+s].
Their baseline per unit of gained interval diverges. This is an exact loss
of normalized gain for the bounded-difference iteration. It does not prove
that its finite residual-gap certificate K_(V_s)<=s+1 always fails. A sequence
could provide enough future surplus despite this ratio. No such comparison
has been established uniformly for the pure-power streams.

## 4. Test of variable gaps against the actual ledger

Allowing growing gaps h_t can avoid the ratio in Section 3, but the gaps must
still start with h_1=1 and obey h_t<=1+sum_(u<t)h_u. An arbitrary large power
cannot be used as the next gap without checking that inequality. A signed
identity also cannot be converted to a positive assignment by discarding its
negative side: its baseline is charged twice in W=2B+H.

The exact retained-index test from move 1 remains

    S_(i-1)-a_i + W-W_(i-1) >= 2B-1,  for every retained i.

After a family is fixed, accepted all-future surplus eventually pays its
constant right side. It does not pay it at all indices after the family was
selected. Selecting a new family changes the right side and the deleted
prefix weights. The attempted use of large, uncontrolled signed identities
therefore leaves precisely the same construction and cost obligations.
No unit identity for every D,k, no full growing-gap family, and no uniform
verification of this retained-index test were produced.

The accepted abstract counterexample in node-000006 should not be cited as
settling a stronger signed-tail variant. Its displayed relation is
b_(2t+1)-3b_(2t)=1, but it supplies only two copies of each value. That relation
alone is not a legal signed unit switch with these occurrence limits. Neither
signed tail coverage nor nearly complete subset sums were proved for that
counterexample in its evidence package.

## 5. Method disposition

The constructive strategy has now used its two permitted step-3 applications.
It produced an exact conditional amplification, excluded bounded-size
bounded-difference tail gadgets, and identified vanishing normalized gain
for disjoint bounded-difference iteration. It has not produced the required
support-compatible seed. The declared no-compatible-seed method failure
applies to this bounded attempt. This is not a theorem that all variable-
length seeds fail, and it is not a counterexample to the root.

Any successor must address a genuinely new mechanism, for example a proved
positive-representation argument from tail signed structure, with its missing
input explicitly stated and independently reviewed. Merely renaming the
unconstructed exchange family or repeating its two applications would not
justify a successor. The full all-D all-positive-cutoff objective remains open.
