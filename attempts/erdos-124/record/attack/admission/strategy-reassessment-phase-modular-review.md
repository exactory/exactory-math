The new claim proposes taking individual correction powers from V while leaving the other powers of those same bases in the reserve. The R-supported signed corrections lie outside V by base label. This differs from an arbitrary finite-block deletion theorem and from changing the constants of a two-whole-reserve argument.

CP26's actual prefix proof supplies a relevant interface. With strict surplus `delta=sigma(V)-1`, its fixed initial charge `C`, gap `g=ceil(C)`, phase tolerance and scale constant `Lambda`, the one-label test requires a new correction value to exceed `Lambda(C+W)`, together with the stated near-alignment conditions. Here W is the weight already removed. The source establishes an all-scale estimate for arbitrary finite selections obeying its tests before specializing to its cyclic scheduling. Thus it is relevant to the proposed modular-only selections. It is not a theorem that an arbitrary signed block of unknown cost may be removed.

The source's prefix and compact-return sections do not use the later spectral gcd argument. This gives a concrete reason to investigate the required V version when gcd(V) is greater than one. It does not excuse the next application from stating and checking that restricted interface. The draft explicitly makes this a proof obligation and lists dependence on an unassumed gcd(V)=1 as a failure signal.

The following obligations remain part of the proposed construction, not accepted conclusions of this review:

1. Fix V's original cutoff, original finite-deletion charge, tolerance, gap and scale constants before selecting any correction. Establish the all-scale invariant on the full admitted V domain.
2. Maintain one W ledger across every correction and every signed unit. Neither the V cutoff nor the ledger may restart when a new unit is built. A later large support cannot determine a replacement gap.
3. Establish phase returns on the actual orbit obtained by restricting correction exponents to multiples of the needed modular period. Compact recurrence on that orbit is the proposed route. An intersection of independently syndetic sets, or a claim of full-torus density without its assumptions, would not suffice.

Modular fullness alone is not a legal signed identity, and coincident numerical values must not collapse distinct labels or permit reuse of one label.

CP22 Section 8 already excludes qualifying radices B at least four and states the restriction on the ternary case. Only B=2 or B=3 can occur.
