## Mathematical substance and initial trigger

The selected entry has a known necessary-condition trigger. Its first
requirement is met by the universal input triple E,k,F and the exact
nonexistence of any legal finite unit at a hypothetical failing input.
An explicit parameter triple does not make that nonexistence a finite
certificate, and the proposal does not say that it does.

The second requirement has substantive support in existing results. The
recorded high-mass theorem applies at
`sigma(E) >= 3 + min{1/4, 2/min E}` with arbitrary positive cutoffs and finite
labelled deletions. The proposal's cancellation of common labels between
consecutive positive representations preserves the permitted signed
coefficients. Thus the stated high-mass exclusion applies to precisely
this witness notion. The ternary proof supplies fresh units on `{3,b}` when
b is coprime to 3. The binary proof supplies them on `{4,8,16,b}` for odd b.
These constructions explicitly advance past finite forbidden supports.
They are applicable positive configurations, not just zero-cutoff examples.

These are existing necessary exclusions on a possible counterexample.
They justify strategy plan step 3, which permits conditions without a finite
range. They do not establish any new stable-tail arithmetic structure or
guarantee that more compression is possible. The study correctly selects
this branch instead of the construction-resource or guessing-principle
branches. Its questions about stable representations and disjoint supports
remain work for reserved moves. I have not answered those questions here.

The recorded native shape also supports a universal quantifier, undecided
direction, absence of a complete argument, and classical ZFC. It explicitly
leaves the constructive ambient operation unknown. There is no need to
replace that unknown with an assumption to enter the selected negative-side
method. At most two extraction applications are proposed, within the
strategy's three-application maximum. A first failure ends the strategy;
two applications are a ceiling, not an entitlement after failure.
