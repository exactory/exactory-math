## Mathematical basis for the decision

CP26 provides a substantive new input for planning. In its actual proof, equations (3)-(4) specify single-power deletions at fixed near-aligned phases. The integer argument in (5), followed by the near, intermediate and far scale estimates (8)-(10), yields

```text
retained strict-prefix total >= x + eta*q - C - W
                            > x + (eta/2)*q.
```

Here q is the latest specially selected power at or below x, and W is the weight of earlier selections. This estimate covers all scales and gives the actual infinite complement the original gap g. It replaces CP25's requirement that the other whole bases retain reciprocal mass at least one. Fixed-neighborhood recurrence on the actual compact orbit closure gives bounded waiting, which preserves every-base exponent spacing. These are properties of a proved occurrence selection, rather than an abstract assertion that a suitable allocation ought to exist.

The proposed preflight asks a more demanding question: can finite interval supports be extracted while a separate reserve retains one fixed gap? CP26 contains no such operation. Its Section8 obtains thickness from

```text
FS_0(R) + FS_0(Q) = FS_0(P).
```

The old R participates in producing these intervals. It cannot then be counted again as an independent reserve. Full Fourier-Stieltjes mean mass on FS_0(Q) also does not make FS_0(Q) thick. There is an especially direct check from the stated recursion: q_i > Lambda(C+W_(i-1)) gives q_i-W_(i-1)>1. The largest differing selection in two distinct finite subsets of Q therefore contributes more than the sum of all smaller possible differences plus one. Those subset sums cannot be consecutive. Thus simply taking T=Q in the proposed partition fails for the actual constructed Q. This observation is a check on the proposed use of the existing proof, not a separate checkpoint or research result.

The draft correctly recognizes that a finite interval witness may use many labels of the old complement. The preservation proof only permits its special single-label steps. An arbitrary witness block changes retained prefix totals at the scales of its own labels. A large later q cannot repair a deficit already created at an earlier retained label. Similarly, applying CP26 again after adding a finite witness to F changes C, and its resulting gap need not remain bounded independently of the extraction stage. Neither termination of the interval-witness search nor finiteness of each support controls that cost. The proposed failure signal explicitly rejects these substitutions.

There is nevertheless a meaningful possible contribution. The accepted Mass proof, Section1, applies to any two disjoint label supplies with a thick subset-sum supply and an infinite reserve of fixed finite gap. Once g is fixed, choose a represented interval [A,A+g-1] in the thick supply. For n>=A+g-1, the reserve supplies a sum in [n-A-g+1,n-A], and the remaining difference is represented on the disjoint interval support. This is exactly the implication the preflight must preserve. A partition on the stated domain would address strict-mass minimum-four eventual completeness, including the unresolved range below the CP24 rung. It would not settle minimum three or reciprocal equality.

The broad partition target is not new: assessment-000001 already states it. Assessment-000002 selected the concrete removable-core construction that became CP25. Assessment-000003 proposed the phase extension as a source question, and assessment-000004 selected its separately admitted operation. CP26 has now supplied that operation's actual preservation proof. The current assessment returns to the original partition target with this new, explicit margin and a narrower question about what it can pay for. That is enough to justify one bounded source preflight. It is insufficient to admit a finite-block operation or to assert that the old support-cost obstruction has been resolved. The draft and manual comparison make precisely this distinction.
