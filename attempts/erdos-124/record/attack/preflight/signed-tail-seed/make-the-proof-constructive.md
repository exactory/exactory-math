## Constraints learned

The value to beat is a qualitative conclusion, not an extremal
constant: eventual fixed-loss central coverage. The previous
counterexample already has label exponent log(4)/log(3), almost-linear
surplus at every prefix, modular fullness and norm divergence. Its
coefficient-three relation is not legal with its two copies. The new
target therefore requires a different, occurrence-respecting mechanism.

Graham's proof amplifies signed identities chosen beyond earlier
supports into longer positive intervals. This supplies a seed-and-
amplify architecture for persistent legal identities, but not a
cofiniteness argument using the same sequence for smoothing.
The paired-perturbation proof supplies a different invariant: two
copies per level with value at least 3^j give a digit-vector upper
bound on numerical support. A proposed combination must preserve
both the invariant and exact signed identities.

Candidate template, still unproved: take two copies per level,
baseline b_j=3^j+floor(3^j/j), j>=2. At sparse endpoints m_t=2^t,
with block starts k_t=m_t-2t, replace the endpoint by
2 sum_(j=k_t)^(m_t-1) b_j-1, for all t beyond an analytically justified
start. The blocks must be disjoint. Each operation appends the next
block and endpoint, leaving past labels unchanged. The point is to
test increasing-support legal relations, not to replay the former
coefficient-three gadget. Ordering, upward perturbation, all-prefix
surplus, norm divergence and numerical support loss remain proof
obligations.

No finite seed enumeration is planned. Prove the extension lemma
first, choose and independently check an analytical seed, apply the
extension, then test the seed class and its limitations, exactly as
seed-and-amplify requires. The target is not extremal, so step 2's
extremal-candidate entry does not apply. No forcing or uncountable
approximation branch is proposed. The strategy permits at most two
step-3 applications and ends on its actual failure signal.
