## Constraints on the next plan

CP24 proves a full Fourier-Stieltjes mean on a gcd-one power supply
without a mass bound. It does not prove that this supply's own subset
sums are thick, or that another supply may be used twice.

A new method must therefore give an exact legal occurrence allocation,
or replace the amplification mechanism itself. A candidate sufficient
hypothesis is the occurrence-level Q,R partition stated in the proposed
formal reassessment: FS(Q) thick, FS(R) syndetic, for every strict-mass
minimum-four input and every cutoff/deletion. Its reserve gap must be
fixed before choosing the interval. This would imply the strict-mass
target by the already accepted amplification argument. It is not known,
and no seed-producing operation was found in this sweep.

The literature's finite-correction argument does not automatically make
an FS-mean correction finite while retaining the conclusion needed for
a reserve. A finite union of supports may alter the subsequent reserve
gap; a limit in mean or arbitrarily high finite density is not a fixed
cofinite representation threshold. These are obligations, not declared
universal impossibility results.

The new rounded-exponential source suggests a separate possible diagnostic:
would adding strict linear surplus to the old abstract profile still
leave explicit incomplete sequences? Its published forced-term mechanism
could test that particular strengthening. This is a question only; no
additional spectral or signed-unit property of any rounded model is
asserted or proved in this study. The generic-profile route is already
retired, so this diagnostic is not automatically preferred over a
power-specific constructive or negative mechanism merely because it may
be easier to finish.
