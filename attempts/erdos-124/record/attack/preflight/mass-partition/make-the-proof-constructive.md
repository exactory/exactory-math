## What the study determines

The route in print is seed amplification, not forcing or generic approximation.
A bounded-gap supply can fill all sufficiently large integers once another
disjoint supply gives an interval of the required finite length. Fan's
finite-support extraction permits the correction component to retain a
bounded-gap remainder. This is the specific new input relative to the
earlier unconstructed switch route.

The proposed symbolic seed is a finite partition of D into three base
classes of reciprocal mass at least one, with gcd one in the correction
class. Its construction must use the accepted minimum-preserving core
and a bounded first-crossing increment, not a finite search over D.
The candidate uniform threshold is 3+2/min D. Optimality is not claimed.

The first seed-and-amplify application must first prove the labelled
amplification criterion, including coincident numerical values, and then
construct the partition and verify the claim for every k,F.
A computable search for a finite interval certificate may be described,
but must not be executed without separate finite-task admission.
The mathematical proof may be non-effective through its cited compactness
and piecewise-Bohr input; no explicit global threshold N is promised.

The target is feasibility at the stated threshold, not optimization, so
the strategy's extremal-candidate step does not apply. The forcing branch
does not apply. A second seed-and-amplify application is allowed only if
the first yields a concrete repairable hypothesis without its failure
signal firing. The first two applications must obey the installed limit.

Failure means that the label extension is invalid, the partition lacks one
of its required masses or its gcd condition, or the finite correction
cannot be separated from the remaining amplification supply.
A failed condition is recorded; no numerical prefix extension follows.
