## Exact statement and canonical comparison

The candidate is the exact minimum-four claim at reciprocal mass251/140, with bases8 and16 additionally required, collective gcd one, every positive k and every finite labelled deletion. It is an infinite structural subfamily of the original objective. It does not purport to cover every minimum-four base set. Keeping coincident values separately is essential, not an optional convention: powers of4,8,16 can coincide numerically while retaining different base/exponent labels.

The accepted quarter-mass theorem covers this subfamily at13/4. The accepted ternary theorem at11/6 applies only when the base3 is present, so it does not already prove this minimum-four candidate. The full root and all uncovered base configurations must remain separate obligations. No finite fraction of the original proof domain is assigned.

## Constraints and missing input

The proposed replacement for balanced ternary is a periodic signed-digit alphabet made from bases4,8,16. Write their values as powers of2 and align a block at an exponent divisible by12, the least common multiple of2,3,4. Repeated numerical weights in that block retain their occurrence multiplicities. The new proof must establish that the signed block realizes a full balanced radix4096 digit interval and that repeated blocks yield arbitrary integer multiples of the aligned power of2. This is a prospective proof obligation, not a verified numerical certificate.

An odd base b exists because4 is present and gcd(D)=1. A high power of b congruent to one modulo the aligned power of2 could then supply legal tail unit alternatives. Both signs, all cutoff inequalities, and avoidance of both alternatives from every earlier pair require proof. Fresh finite copies would feed the predecessor's interval/reserve interface.

The reserve is all bases outside {4,8,16,b}. The proposed uniform threshold is chosen to leave mass one after the seed bases and the worst possible odd b>=5 are removed. The arithmetic and equality case must be checked analytically. No part of this plan can infer completeness from signed units without its separate reserve.
