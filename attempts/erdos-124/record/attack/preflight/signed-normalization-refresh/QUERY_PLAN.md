## Exact research question

For every finite nonempty gcd-one set E of distinct integer bases at
least3 with sigma(E)=sum_e 1/(e-1)>=1/2, every positive cutoff and every
finite labelled deletion, can one construct a finite signed sum equal
to1 using coefficients only in {-1,0,1}? The unresolved mixed-base
portion excludes the previously handled common-radix half-mass families;
CP26 now also supplies units when min E>=4 and sigma(E)>1.

The purpose is to locate an actual nonlinear, target-dependent or
cross-base normalization operation, or another source-backed tool
controlling the least positive signed tail value independently of the
cutoff. Universal expanding scalars preserving each base separately are
already obstructed by node15 move2. Gcd1, full modular coverage and a
cutoff-dependent signed-gap bound are insufficient, as its move1 shows.
These exact earlier proofs have been reread before this follow-up.

No mathematical process or entry is authorized. A discovered method
must preserve each occurrence coefficient and every cutoff, avoid the
recorded scalar obstruction, and have explicit gain and failure tests
before a new reviewed proposal. A double-base product d^a e^b is not
an available pure-power occurrence. Nor does subtracting two signed
representations preserve coefficient capacity automatically.
