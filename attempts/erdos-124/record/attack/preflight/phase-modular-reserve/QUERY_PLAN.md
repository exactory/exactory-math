Date: 2026-09-08. Controller snapshot387. This is a distinct structural completeness question after the protected-block and general signed-normalization preflights. Neither earlier study admitted mathematics on this claim.

## Claim to investigate

For every nonempty finite set D of distinct integers at least 3 with gcd(D)=1 for which there exist an integer B>=2 and a nonempty finite set A of positive integers such that R={B^a:a in A} is contained in D, sigma(R)=sum_{d in R}1/(d-1)>=1/2, V=D minus R has min V>=4 and sigma(V)>1, every integer k>=1 and every finite set F of labelled occurrences (d,j) with d in D and j>=k admit an integer N(D,k,F) such that every integer n>=N is a sum of finitely many remaining occurrences d^j, each pair (d,j) used at most once and coincident numerical values from different pairs retained separately.

## Candidate operation, not a proof

Choose the gap g from V,k,F before constructing any interval. For each fresh signed unit choose a common-radix tail modulus B^L. Seek finitely many V correction labels with sum congruent to1 modulo B^L, taking them one at a time at CP26-admissible near-aligned phases and above its scale threshold depending on previous removed weight. Restrict each exponent to suitable multiples so that its residue is stable at every prime-power divisor of B^L, including bases sharing factors with B. The signed R lattice must supply the remaining multiple of B^L on fresh labels. Repeat for g-1 disjoint pairs and amplify against the actual unused V supply.

Success requires a single gap g independent of all later corrections, legal recurrence on those exponent multiples, finite completion, and all-cutoff finite-deletion disjointness. CP26's gap lemma on V must be justified without assuming gcd(V)=1, since its later spectral theorem has that extra assumption. The proposed result would include min D=3 and total mass strictly above3/2 by taking R={3}, but that implication is still part of the proposed deduction. It does not cover equality sigma(V)=1 or the original all-mass-one target. No arbitrary-block cost or generic equidistribution is assumed.
