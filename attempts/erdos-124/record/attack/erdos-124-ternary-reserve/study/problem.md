## Exact scope and comparison

The proposed attack concerns every finite set D of distinct integer bases at least three with min D=3, gcd(D)=1 and sigma(D)>=11/6. Every positive exponent cutoff k and every finite deletion of occurrence labels remain universally quantified. Equal numerical powers retain distinct labels. This is a genuine special case of the original threshold-one objective, not a replacement for it.

The accepted quarter-mass result covers this minimum only at sigma(D)>=13/4. The new threshold is a candidate for a materially wider range. No percentage of the original infinite-domain proof is assigned. The canonical positive-cutoff distinction remains as in the existing saved canonical-124-reading.txt in the mass-partition study; the cached database responses and BEGL96 reread are consistent with it. Neither a zero-cutoff proof nor a cutoff-one calculation proves this candidate.

## Constraints on the plan

Classical balanced ternary supplies a prospective signed representation mechanism only for base three. Since gcd(D)=1 and three belongs to D, some other base b is not divisible by three. A congruence b^J=1 modulo a sufficiently large power of three is a possible way to put a signed unit relation beyond any prescribed cutoff. The actual proof must verify the congruence, balanced digits, positive and negative supports, and avoidance of every previously used label.

The new hypothesis is that iterating these variable-length, disjoint tail unit pairs produces intervals of every finite length using only bases three and b. The independent reserve consists of all other bases. Its mass is the exact quantity sigma(D)-1/2-1/(b-1), which is required to be at least one. The proposed threshold 11/6 must be checked using b>=4. The interval/reserve lemma is already available from the accepted mass proof, but the seed is not.

This supplies the ingredient absent from the failed carry-seed branch: a concrete variable-length construction and a separate reserve whose deficit bound does not depend on the growing seed support. It does not revive the refuted inference from signed units alone to completeness. Thickness alone does not imply the root claim. The method makes no claim for a base set not containing three or for all inputs below the proposed threshold.
