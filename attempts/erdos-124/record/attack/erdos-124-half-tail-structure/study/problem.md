# Problem study: arbitrary-base half-mass signed tail units

The exact candidate is:

For every nonempty finite set E of distinct integers at least 3 with gcd(E)=1 and sigma(E)=sum_{d in E}1/(d-1)>=1/2, every integer k>=1 and every finite set F of labelled occurrences (d,j) with d in E and j>=k, there exist disjoint finite subsets P and Q of the remaining labels such that sum_{(d,j) in Q}d^j-sum_{(d,j) in P}d^j=1; each labelled pair is used at most once, and coincident numerical values from different pairs remain separately available.

## Constraints learned

The new threshold one half is suggested by the factor two in the signed
prefix inequality. This motivation is not an arbitrary-cutoff proof.
The zero-exponent alphabet contains unit labels, whereas a positive cutoff
removes those labels. An unrestricted Bezout representation, a bounded
signed gap, modular density or a real Cantor interval does not by itself
give a finite signed representation with coefficient at most one per label.

The accepted generic signed-tail counterexample shows that even obtaining
units would not alone imply positive eventual coverage. Any contribution
back to the root must keep a disjoint mass-one reserve and prove its
allocation separately. The previous unconstructed support-cost exchanges
and generic two-component source route are not being restarted.

## Exact root connection to be reviewed

A proposed separate core statement asks whether every gcd-one D with
sigma(D)>=2 contains a gcd-one E with 1/2<=sigma(E)<1. If both that
statement and the present half-mass unit statement hold, D minus E has
mass greater than one. Its positive subset sums have bounded gaps. Fresh
unit alternatives from E would then build an interval of that fixed gap
length without consuming reserve labels, giving the original conclusion
at sigma(D)>=2. A separate explicit implication bridge must certify this
deduction. The original complementary range 1<=sigma(D)<2 remains open.
Neither the core statement nor this bridge is treated as already accepted.

## Selected question and stop

The selected question is the exact finite-support tail-unit assertion,
not all signed integer coverage, a uniform support bound, a finite cutoff
test or a positive completeness theorem without a reserve. The full new
claim has no proof or counterexample yet. The bounded source count in
QUERY_LOG.md has been reached, with no exhaustive novelty claim.
