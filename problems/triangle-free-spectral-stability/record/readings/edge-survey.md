# Edge Survey

## Problem

Survey edge-spectral Turan extremal problems, which maximize adjacency spectral radius for a fixed number of edges under a forbidden-subgraph constraint.

## Claims

The survey records Nosal equality, the two-largest-eigenvalues bound, the nonbipartite triangle-free gap lambda<=sqrt(m-1), sharper extremizers for fixed edge parity, and extensive results for cycles, theta graphs, fans, wheels and color-critical forbidden graphs. It lists open extensions and unresolved small or parity cases. Theorem 4.6 gives the general asymptotic edge-spectral Turan result; Theorem 4.7 records a color-critical exact extremal result.

## Assumptions

The surveyed statements have different hypotheses on forbidden graphs, edge parity and sufficiently large edge counts. The default graph class excludes isolates, but isolates do not affect the edge count or nonzero adjacency eigenvalues. Individual conditions are retained and are not pooled into one theorem.

## Methods

This is a survey of extremal statements and constructions, not a new proof of each theorem. It compares edge and vertex spectral problems, blow-up constructions, subdivisions of complete bipartite graphs, and recently obtained edge-spectral stability applications.

## Limitations

The source predates the September/October 2026 bipartite coefficient-three lemma, so it cannot establish the present state of that bound. A survey statement is not a full reading of the original proof it cites. It does not formulate the selected edit-distance conjecture, and absence from this survey alone gives no openness guarantee.

## Relevance

It supports that edge-spectral extremal graph theory is an active line and distinguishes fixed-edge extremal radius questions from quantitative complete-bipartite editing. It supplies relevant neighbours and sources without replacing exact-statement searches.
