# Entropy Bridge

## Problem

Transfer weighted Turan inequalities into spectral inequalities for walks and tree homomorphisms, with color-critical forbidden graphs.

## Claims

Theorem 3.1 gives an exact entropy identity with two relative-entropy remainders. Theorems 5.1 and 5.2 prove weighted supersaturation and qualitative weighted stability; Theorem 1.8 gives an exact weighted density bound for sufficiently flat vectors and color-critical forbidden graphs. Theorem 1.1 settles the walk-spectral color-critical conjecture except the necessarily excluded pair (r,ell)=(2,2). Appendix A states general qualitative edge-spectral stability, proves the r>=3 part, and explicitly quotes the r=2 part from arXiv:2508.15271 rather than proving it by the new method.

## Assumptions

The entropy bridge uses a positive unit Perron vector on a connected component with edges. Weighted results impose a small maximum probability weight. The spectral flatness argument needs a fixed gap above one half; stars prevent that flatness for even walk lengths at equality. Appendix A requires r>=3 in both its flatness step and its three-part core argument.

## Methods

A Perron Markov chain and Gibbs inequality yield the entropy identity. Rational blow-ups, graph removal, stability, random partition rounding and weighted sampling prove the weighted results. Appendix A uses near-equality in Cauchy-Schwarz to control edge coordinate products and then converts weights to unweighted editing on an almost regular core.

## Evidence

Read all forty-one pages of extracted text, all seventy references and the entire post-bibliography Appendix A. Inspected native PDF Table 1 on page 3, Figure 1 on page 5 and Theorem A.1 with Lemma A.2 on page 39. The explicit exclusion of a new r=2 appendix proof was checked in both text and PDF.

## Limitations

The paper supplies no coefficient-three or uniform linear complete-bipartite edit bound for triangle-free graphs. Its edge-spectral appendix does not newly treat r=2. The entropy identity alone measures weighted distributional errors; turning those into unweighted edit counts requires additional estimates. No historical novelty conclusion follows from the absence of the proposed bound in this one paper.

## Relevance

A decisive recent alternative-method comparator for openness and routine-route assessment. It settles a neighbouring walk-count conjecture, but leaves the selected quantitative triangle-free editing question unaddressed in the statements and proofs inspected.
