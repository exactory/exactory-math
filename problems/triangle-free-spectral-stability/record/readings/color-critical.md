# Color Critical

## Problem

Determine fixed-edge adjacency spectral extremizers for color-critical and several bipartite forbidden graphs using edge-spectral stability.

## Claims

Theorem 1.5 gives the exact large-size bound for color-critical forbidden graphs of chromatic number at least four. Theorems 1.8 and 1.9 treat almost-bipartite forbidden graphs with nearly split extremizers. Theorem 2.1 recalls qualitative edge-spectral edit stability; Lemma 2.3 bounds Perron-vector perturbation by the square root or fourth root of edit distance divided by spectral-gap factors. Theorem 3.1 turns a close multipartite approximation into an exact spectral extremal theorem under its additional part-size and forbidden-subgraph hypotheses.

## Assumptions

Finite simple graphs. Large edge count, forbidden graph, chromatic number and part-size assumptions vary by theorem and are essential. The exact extremal switching arguments assume an extremal spectral threshold, rather than an arbitrary positive spectral deficit.

## Methods

Qualitative edge-spectral stability supplies a multipartite approximation. Perron-vector perturbation and local switching identify exact extremizers. The almost-bipartite analysis controls exceptional vertices and uses an eigenvalue expansion for joins with a fixed graph.

## Limitations

This is not a theorem bounding complete-bipartite edit distance linearly in m-lambda^2 for every triangle-free graph. Its local exact argument cannot be applied at a positive deficit without controlling the losses. Conjecture 5.2 is addressed by the separately read entropy-bridge paper, so it must not be presented as presently open merely because this paper poses it.

## Relevance

A decisive comparator for the stability-to-exactness route and a source of a concrete alternative method. Its quantitative perturbation losses and exact-threshold hypotheses distinguish it from the selected coefficient-three editing question.
